Tibok Tibok

If you’ve ever had Maja Blanca, imagine its Kapampangan cousin. That’s Tibok-Tibok. The name literally translates to "heartbeat," derived from the subtle, gentle bubbling of the milk as it slowly thickens on the stove, almost resembling a soft pulse. Unlike standard coconut milk puddings, authentic Tibok-Tibok is built almost entirely on fresh carabao’s milk (gatas ng damulag). Because water buffalo milk has nearly twice the butterfat of cow's milk, it yields a lush texture with a delicate and slightly tangy taste. Infused with a hint of citrus from dayap (local lime) peel and topped with crispy and toasted latik (coconut curds), it is pure and a highlight dessert found in our kitchen.

Ingredients

    Pudding's Base

  • Fresh Carabao's Milk (Substitute: Full Cream Cow's Milk): 4 cups
  • Cornstarch: 1/2 cup
  • White Sugar: 3/4 cups (adjust to preferred sweetness)
  • Dayap (Substitute: Lime Zest): 1 strip
  • Fine Salt: 1 pinch

    • Toppings and Finish

  • Fresh Coconut Cream (For Latik): 2 cups, simmered down to yield golden latik curds and coconut oil for greasing

    • Liquids

  • Cooking Oil

Special Halo-Halo

Halo-halo, meaning “mix-mix” in Filipino, is a beloved Filipino dessert made for hot days and sweet cravings. It brings together colorful layers of sweetened fruits, beans, jellies, coconut, and shaved ice, finished with creamy milk and indulgent toppings. Every spoonful offers a little bit of everything.May it be icy, creamy, chewy, fruity, and sweet. Often served in a tall glass, halo-halo is meant to be mixed together before eating, creating a delicious "mix" of flavors and textures in every bite.

Ingredients

    Colorful Base Layers

  • Ube Halaya: spread on the sides of the cup
  • Green and Red Gulaman
  • Sweetened Saba Bananas
  • Sweetened Red Mung Beans and White Kidney Beans
  • Nata de Coco and Kaong
  • Macapuno Strips
  • Sweetened Jackfruit (Langka)

    • The Ice and Cream

  • Finely Shaved Ice: 2-3 cups
  • Evaporated Milk: 1/2 cup, drizzled over the shaved ice

    • Toppings

  • Ube Ice Cream: 1 scoop
  • Leche Flan: 1 slice
  • Toasted Crispy Pounded Rice (Pinipig)
  • Ube Halaya

Turon

If you walk past any busy Filipino street corner in the afternoon, the unmistakable scent of deep-fried caramel will instantly draw you in. Turon (sweet banana spring rolls) is undeniable one of the Filipino's well-known afternoon snack (meryenda). A golden, crunchy envelope wrapped around riped saba bananas and sweet jackfruit, that are then glazed with a crackling shell of caramelized brown sugar. While it’s famous as a quick street bite, making it at home lets you achieve that perfect balance for your own taste. An outer wrapper that is crunchy, which gives way to a warm, gooey, and sweet filling inside. It’s simple, nostalgic, and impossible to eat just one.

Ingredients

    Fillings and Wrapper

  • Riped Saba Banana: peeled and sliced in half lengthwise
  • Riped Jackfruit (Langka): optional but adds texture and sweetness
  • Lumpia or Spring Roll Wrappers

    • Sweet Coating and Fry

  • Brown Sugar or Muscovado Sugar: for caramelizing
  • Cooking Oil
  • Cornstach Mixture or Egg Whites: for sealing the wrappers

Sampelot (Bilo-Bilo)

When rainy afternoons roll in, there’s nothing quite like holding a warm bowl of Sampelot. While most Filipinos know this sweet coconut dessert as Ginataang Bilo-Bilo, the Kapampangan people call it Sampelot. A thick stew packed with chewy rice balls, tapioca pearls, and sweet fruits, all gently simmered in velvety coconut cream. It truly is the ultimate comfort in a bowl, especially during cool and rainy seasons. Every spoonful brings a delightful mix of textures. The chewy, handmade glutinous rice balls (bilo-bilo), cubes of sweet purple yam and sweet potato, soft saba bananas, and translucent tapioca pearls. Infused with the sweetness of riped jackfruit (langka), it’s a soul-warming treat that reminds us how simple fruits and convenient ingredients that can be found in many Filipino's kitchens can create a warm and sweet dish that brings comfort to many.

Ingredients

    The Bilo-Bilo (Sticky Rice Balls)

  • Glutinous Rice Flour: 1 1/2 cups
  • Water: 3/4 cup (or more, as needed to form smooth dough)

    • Coconut Stew Base

  • Coconut Milk (Gata): 3 cups
  • Heavy Coconut Cream (Kakang Gata): 1 1/2 cups
  • Brown Sugar or Granulated Sugar: 3/4 cup
  • Salt: 1 pinch

    • Produce and Fillings

  • Yellow Sweet Potato (Kamote): 1 cup, peeled and cubed
  • Ube Halaya or Taro (Gabi): 1 cup, peeled and cubed
  • Saba Bananas: 2 medium-sized, sliced into rounds
  • Ripe Jackfruit (Langka): 1/2 cup, thin strips
  • Tapioca Pearls or Sago: 1/2 cup, cooked and drained
