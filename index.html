Famous Kapampangan Sisig

Kapampangan sisig is a beloved Filipino dish that originated in Pampanga, known as the culinary capital of the Philippines. Traditionally made from finely chopped pig’s head and chicken liver, it is seasoned with calamansi, onions, and chili peppers, creating a savory, tangy, slightly spicy dish with a rich and crispy texture. Often served sizzling on a hot plate, Kapampangan sisig is a flavorful example of Pampanga’s resourceful and distinctive culinary tradition.

Ingredients

    Meats

  • Pig's Face (Ears, Snout, Cheeks): 1 kg
  • Chicken Liver: 250g

    • Aromatics and Seasonings

  • Red Onion: 1 piece medium-sized, finely diced
  • Siling Labuyo: 3-5 pieces
  • Fresh Calamansi: 5-6 pieces, squeezed or juiced
  • Rock Salt: Adjust to taste:
  • Crushed Black Pepper: 1 tsp

    • Optional Toppings

  • Egg: 1 raw egg, cracked on a sizzling plate (Optional, traditional Kapampangan Sisig contains no egg)

    • Liquids

  • Cooking Oil

Bringhe

If paella had a soulful, coconut-infused Filipino cousin, it would be Bringhe. Rooted in the culinary capital of Pamapanga, this vibrant, golden-hued rice dish is the ultimate celebration on a plate, traditionally served at town fiestas, family reunions, and holiday feasts. Unlike standard fried rice, Bringhe is built on a rich foundation of glutinous rice (malagkit) simmered low and slow in aromatic coconut milk and freshly grated turmeric. The result? A deeply savory, slightly sweet base with a luxurious, sticky texture that absorbs every drop of flavor.

Ingredients

    Rice Base

  • Glutinous Rice (Malagkit): 1 1/2 cups
  • Jasmine or White Rice: 1/2 cup

    • Liquids and Aromatics

  • Coconut Milk: 2 cups
  • Chicken Broth (or Water): 1 cup
  • Yellow Onion: 1 whole, finely chopped
  • Turmeric Juice or Turmeric Powder: 1 tbsp
  • Garlic: 4 cloves, minced
  • Fresh Ginger: 1 thumb-sized piece, cut into thin sticks
  • Cooking Oil

    • Meat and Seasonings

  • Chicken Thighs or Drumsticks: 500g
  • Chorizo de Bilbao: 2 pieces
  • Chicken Liver: 100g
  • Fish Sauce: 2 tbsp (adjust to taste)
  • Ground Black Pepper: 1/2 tsp

    • Mix-ins and Garnish

  • Red Bell Pepper: Small-sized, sliced to strips
  • Frozen Green Peas: 1/3 cup
  • Raisins: 1/3 cup
  • Hard Boiled Eggs: sliced to wedges (optional, can be cut into own style)

    • Wrap and Liner

  • Banana Leaves: 2-3 large-sized, lightly wilted over open flame (for wrapping)

Pinakbet

While the northern Ilocano original is all about steamed simplicity and pungent fish paste, the Kapampangan adaptation turns this humble vegetable dish into a rich, aromatic gisa (sauté). In Pampanga’s food-loving kitchens, sweet yellow squash (kalabasa) melts into a silky sauce, sautéed tomatoes release their natural tang, and savory bagoong alamang (fermented shrimp paste) adds a deep, mouthwatering umami background.

Ingredients

    Meat and Aromatics

  • Pork Belly (Liempo): 250g
  • Sautéed Shrimp Paste(Bagoong Alamang): 3 tbsp
  • Yellow Onion: 1 medium-sized, diced
  • Garlic: 4 cloves, minced
  • Riped Tomatoes: 3 medium-sized, chopped
  • Fish Sauce: 1 tsp (adjust to taste)
  • Ground Black Pepper: 1/2 tsp
  • Cooking Oil

    • Vegetables

  • Squash (Kalabasa): 2 cups, peeled and cut-into 1 inch cubes
  • Bitter Gourd(Ampalaya): 1 medium-sized piece, cored and sliced
  • Asian Eggplants: 2 medium-sized pieces
  • Okra: 8-10 pieces, trimmed stems
  • Yardlong Beans (Sitaw): 1 bundle, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • Ground Black Pepper: 1/2 tsp

Kare-kare

If you peek into my home on a Sunday morning, the rich aroma of freshly roasted peanuts and toasted rice is my absolute favorite sign that something special is bubbling on the stove. Around here, nothing brings family together quite like a pot of traditional Kapampangan Kare-Kare. This dish is a warm, golden hug in a bowl, tender beef oxtail and tripe slow-simmered until melt-in-your-mouth soft, then bathed in a silky, earthy peanut sauce colored with bright annatto. Left deliberately unseasoned, the stew relies on a generous side of pungent, sautéed bagoong alamang to bring every bite to life.

Ingredients

    Meat and Broth

  • Beef Oxtail: 1kg
  • Beef Tripe: 500g
  • Yellow Onion: 1 medium-sized, chopped
  • Garlic: 5 cloves, minced
  • Water: 8-10 cups (for braising)

    • Signature Peanut Sauce

  • Unsweetened Ground Roasted Peanuts: 1 cup
  • Glutinous Rice: 1/2 cups, toasted until golden and ground into fine powder
  • Cooking Oil: 2 tbsp
  • Annatto Seeds (Atsuete): 3 tbsp, soaked in 1/4 cup warm water (for dye)
  • Reserved Beef Braising Broth: 3 cups

    • Vegetables

  • Banana Heart (Puso ng Saging): 1 small-sized, outer layers removed, sliced to wedges, and pre-soaked in salted water
  • Asian Eggplants: 2 medium-sized pieces, sliced diagonally
  • Asian Eggplants: 2 medium-sized pieces
  • Pechay or Bok Choy: 2 bunches, trimmed edge
  • Yardlong Beans (Sitaw): 1 bundle, cut into 2-inch pieces

    • For Serving

  • Sautéed Shrimp Paste (Bagoong Alamang): served alongside
